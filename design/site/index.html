Design draft

An eight-track groovebox that a Move owner already knows how to play

Feverdream runs on the Ableton Move in place of Move's own software. Every Move gesture keeps its meaning; everything new is reached by a gesture Move already taught. This site is the design plan, laid out so you can see how the parts connect. Nothing here is built yet.

A track is a belt of devices

The wheel moves the belt, a press enters the device in the middle, Back leaves. Inside, the wheel turns pages: the name for saving and loading, the main page that is the device's own world, then plain pages of eight. Try it.

Keys: ← → turn the wheel, Enter presses, Esc is Back, hold Shift. Drag a knob up or down to turn it; a touch alone shows its name.

The belt, wherever kinds are chosen

After EvilGenius: boxes of pixel art on a conveyor, the chosen one large in the middle, its name under the belt. The chain is one, running into MIX. Session Mode's wheel is another, running into OUT. Pad layouts, track types, device kinds and tools are proposed.

The screen: big, one thing, told on touch

The panel is about three centimetres wide, so nothing depends on small text. At rest it is a picture. A touched knob or wheel takes the whole screen and says what it is. One 5 × 7 face at three sizes, three lines at most. These are hand-drawn mock-ups of the rules, not the instrument's renderer.

One grammar for everything

Nine rules, three sentence shapes and one hold window. Every other part of the design applies them.

  1. Move firstEvery gesture in the Move manual does the same thing here.
  2. One grammar for everything newSelect then turn. Hold a thing then turn or press. Modifier plus target.
  3. Three questions always answeredWhere am I? What do the controls do now? What just happened?
  4. Modifiers explain themselvesA held modifier shows a hint card and lights its targets amber.
  5. No hidden modesA lit button and a corner symbol for every state. Back goes up a level.
  6. Two hands, never threePlayed in the lap: modifier under one hand, target under the other.
  7. Everything can be saidWords come first; the screen draws them and the voice says them.
  8. Big, simple, told on touchA picture at rest; a touched control takes the screen.
  9. Modulation is seenA modulated value is drawn moving; a touched knob shows the value being set.

Press it: quick click, hold, double click

A press lights the button and nothing more. A hold acts when the 300 ms window ends. A quick click acts on release, or after the double-click window when the control has a double click. Nothing belonging to one ever shows for another.

Eight tracks on four buttons

Bank A is tracks 1 to 4 in warm colours, bank B is 5 to 8 in cool ones. Shift + a track button selects the same row in the other bank. While Shift is held the buttons already show where you would land.

Sequencing: Move's steps, with RemoteFM1's depth

Sixteen steps a page, up to sixteen pages a clip, a rate for each clip, and a TRIG page for what a white light cannot show: chance, conditions, ratchets, micro timing and functions that move the playhead.

Pages and length

The TRIG page

The first page of the sequencer after its piano roll, with RemoteFM1's knob order. No step held: defaults for steps entered next. A step held: that step.

EncoderValueRange
1 MICROmicro timing; the same value as step + Left / Righthalf a step early to half a step late
2 RATCHhits spread over the step1 to 8
3 CHANCEprobability the step plays1 to 100%

How it gets built

A portable C core with three faces: the Move, a headless simulator for tests, and a browser build. The browser build is why the whole interface can be clicked through and judged before any sound exists.

Layers

plat/movestatic aarch64 binary: the instrument
plat/simheadless, driven by scripts of presses: tests
plat/webWebAssembly with a drawing of the Move: the desk, then the Remote
appties the layers together; owns the threads' handoff
ctlgestures, one hold window, modes, actions
uiscreen, LEDs, words, belt, themes, motion
webmanager server, remote protocol
modelprojects, tracks, clips, notes, locks, devices, undo
seqclock, launch, sequencers
dspmodule host, instruments, effects, mixer; portable C, later Max for Live

One description of a parameter

Written once in the schema, with a stable id, and read by everything that touches a value.

fd_param→ encodertouch viewvoicestep lock automationmacromod matrixpreset webLive export

A page is a name and up to eight parameter ids, so a parameter can sit on a device's main page and on its own page, and pages can be rearranged without breaking a project.

Order of work

Each stage ends with something to play with and judge. Stage 1 has no sound on purpose: the risk is clarity, and that is cheapest to test with no engine underneath.
